What Mailwarden does

A cluttered Gmail inbox usually holds tens of thousands of messages but only 150–400 distinct bulk senders — newsletters, notifications, promotions, receipts. Mailwarden groups your inbox by sender so you make roughly 200 decisions instead of 20,000.

For each sender it shows the evidence behind its verdict: how many messages, how many you have opened, how far back they go, whether the sender offers a one-click unsubscribe. You choose what to archive, what to trash, and what to leave alone. Mailwarden then carries out exactly what you approved and gives you a receipt you can reverse.

How it works

  1. Scan. Mailwarden reads message metadata — never message content — and groups your inbox by sender.
  2. Review. Every sender is classified and shown to you with the reasoning. Nothing is hidden behind a summary.
  3. Approve. You select what should happen. Nothing moves until you press a button on a screen listing exactly what will move.
  4. Undo. Every batch is recorded with each message's prior labels, so it can be reversed exactly.

What Mailwarden accesses, and why

Mailwarden requests the smallest set of Google permissions that lets it do the job, and no others.

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.modify

Lets Mailwarden read message metadata and move messages — archive, apply labels, or send to Trash — once you have approved a batch. This permission deliberately cannot permanently delete mail. Mailwarden never requests https://mail.google.com/, the full-access permission that can. Anything Mailwarden trashes stays in your Gmail Trash for 30 days, where you can restore it yourself without Mailwarden's help.

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/userinfo.email

Identifies which Google account you signed in with, so your settings and history belong to you and nobody else.

Mailwarden stores sender addresses, message dates, sizes, and salted hashes — never message bodies, never readable subject lines, never recipient addresses. Message content is never written to its database, never logged, and never sent to an AI model. Disconnecting revokes Mailwarden's access to your Google account and purges its stored metadata within 24 hours.

Full detail is in the privacy policy. Mailwarden's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What Mailwarden will not touch

These are protected automatically and cannot be overridden by a misclassification:

  • Anything you have starred
  • Anything with an attachment
  • Threads you have replied to
  • Receipts, invoices, and order confirmations
  • Security mail — login codes, password resets, verification messages

Protection is deliberately over-inclusive. Missing some clutter is recoverable; losing a receipt is not.

Who runs Mailwarden

Mailwarden is an independent product currently in a closed beta limited to 100 users — the cap Google places on an application whose OAuth verification is still in progress. Questions, access requests, and data deletion requests go to mkb.kalra@gmail.com.

Mailwarden cannot permanently delete anything. Every action is reversible for 30 days.
