How it works

1 · Scan. Headers only — sender, date, size, and Gmail's own labels. Message bodies are never part of the scan.

2 · Understand. Your inbox collapses from tens of thousands of messages to a few hundred senders. That is the unit you actually decide about.

3 · Protect. Starred mail, conversations you replied to, receipts, login codes, bank and travel mail, attachments, and anything Mailwarden does not understand are all held back automatically.

4 · Review. One sender at a time, with the evidence in view — unread rate, reply history, how long since they last wrote.

5 · Clean. Nothing moves until you approve it. Then you get a receipt and an Undo button that works for 30 days.

Safe by design

  • It cannot permanently delete anything. Google does not grant Mailwarden that ability with the access it uses — this is enforced by the permission itself, not by a promise.
  • Archive over trash. Archived mail stays in All Mail forever. Trash is offered, but it is the only action with a deadline and it is labelled as one.
  • Everything is reversible for 30 days, and undo is verified — Mailwarden reads Gmail back to confirm the restore rather than assuming it worked.
  • When Mailwarden isn't sure, it doesn't touch it. Missing some clutter is an acceptable cost. Touching something you needed is not.

Your email is yours

  • Sorting uses headers only — sender, date, size, Gmail's labels.
  • Subjects are stored as a salted hash, never as readable text.
  • A message is fetched only when you open it, and is never saved.
  • No message content is ever sent to an AI model. Classification runs on aggregate statistics about senders.
  • Disconnect any time and everything is erased within 24 hours.

Beta note: while Google's review runs, access is capped at 100 users and you may occasionally be asked to reconnect.
